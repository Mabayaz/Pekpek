I. SWOT ANALYSIS
Strengths: The biggest strength in this case was the actual proof. Usually, when someone
does something bad, it is just one person’s word against another. But here, the good guys had the
real Facebook messages. The bad guy typed out exactly what he wanted the 14-year-old girl to
do, asking her to take off her clothes on camera. Because it was all written down on the internet,
he could not lie and say, "I never said that."
Another huge strength was the kind of rule the bad guy broke. In law, there is a big rule
called malum prohibitum. To explain it simply: think of a rule at home like "no jumping on the
bed." If you jump on the bed, you are in trouble. It does not matter if you were jumping to catch
a balloon, or if you thought it was a joke. The rule is the rule. The law against asking kids for bad
pictures works exactly the same way. The judges didn't have to prove the bad guy wanted to sell
the pictures. Just asking for them was enough to break the rule. Lastly, it was very easy to prove
the girl was a child because her birth certificate showed she was only 14.

Weaknesses: The biggest tricky part was how the mom found the secret messages. The
mom saw the girl's phone, took it, and forced her to open the bad guy's Facebook account. The
bad guy tried to use this to escape trouble. He told the judge, "Hey! The mom broke my secret
password and snooped on my phone! That's not fair, so you can't use those messages against
me!" If the judge had agreed that the mom cheated by looking, the bad guy would have gone
free.
Another tricky part was the little girl's feelings. When she talked to the judge, she said, "I
thought it was just a silly joke. I wasn't scared or hurt." Because she did not feel hurt, the first
judge actually said the bad guy was not guilty of one of the charges (the general child abuse
charge). The lawyers had to work extra hard to show that even if the child thought it was a game,
it was still a very serious crime.

Opportunities: This case was a perfect chance for the biggest judges in the land (the
Supreme Court) to make a super important rule for all parents. They used this case to loudly tell
everyone: "Moms and dads are allowed to look at phones to keep their kids safe." This gives
parents the superpower to check on their kids without worrying that they are breaking the law.
It also gave the judges a chance to explain the "Data Privacy" rule. Data privacy is supposed to
keep our information safe from strangers. But the judges made it clear: you are absolutely
allowed to share someone's secret information if you are doing it to catch a bad guy and protect a
child in court.
Threats: Even though the good guys won this time, there are still big threats in the
world. The biggest threat is that technology changes so fast. Bad guys are getting smarter. They
use secret apps that make pictures and messages disappear forever after 10 seconds. If the bad
guy in this story had used a disappearing app, the mom would never have found the evidence,
and the bad guy would have gotten away with it.
Another scary threat is that bad guys will keep trying to use the law to hide. They will keep
crying, "But my privacy! But my secrets!" Courts always have to stay awake and alert so they
don't accidentally let a bad guy go free just because he used a password.

II. LEGAL PRINCIPLES
1. The Big Privacy Rule is Only for the Police The Constitution (the biggest rulebook
of the country) says everyone has a right to keep their letters and messages private. But the
judges explained a very simple concept: this rule is only meant to stop the police or
the government from spying on you for no reason. It is NOT a rule to stop a mom from checking
her daughter's phone. A mom protecting her child is not the police, so she is allowed to find the
truth.
2. The "No Excuses" Rule (Malum Prohibitum) As mentioned before, some rules care
about why you did something. But this special law does not care about your "why." The principle
here is simple: asking a child for naked pictures is so dangerous and so bad that there are zero
excuses allowed. You can't say "we were dating," or "I was just keeping them for myself." The
act itself is the crime.
3. A Child's "Yes" Does Not Count One of the most important rules in the world is that
children cannot say "yes" to dangerous adult things. The bad guy tried to say, "But she said yes!
She agreed to it!" The law says a child's brain is still growing. They do not understand how scary
the internet is or that a picture can be shared with the whole world. Because they don't
understand the danger, the law steps in like a giant shield and says, "A child's 'yes' is basically a
'no.'"

III. ANALYSIS (PERSONAL)
When I look at this whole story, I think the big judges made the smartest and best choice
possible. Today, kids practically live inside their phones and iPads. They talk, play, and go to
school on screens. This makes it super easy for bad guys to hide behind their own screens and
trick kids.
What I find really interesting is how the bad guy tried to play a trick on the judges. He
basically said, "You can't punish me, because the mom broke the rules by reading my private
messages!" He tried to use the country's biggest rulebook to hide his bad actions. I am so glad the
judges said "No way!" If the judges had listened to him, it would mean parents could never look
at their kids' phones. It would mean the bad guys could always hide behind passwords. The
judges made sure that a child's safety is always, always more important than a bad guy's
password.
I also really like how the judges handled the "we were boyfriend and girlfriend" excuse.
It is normal for teenagers to have crushes. The 14-year-old girl in this story really thought the
guy liked her and thought sending pictures was just a silly joke. She didn't know that once a
picture goes on the internet, it can never be deleted. The judges drew a giant line in the sand.
They said it is the adult's job to be responsible. It doesn't matter if the teenager thinks it's a
joke—the adult should know better. The court acted like a giant protector, showing that nobody
is ever allowed to trick a child, no matter what excuse they use.

IV. COURT RULING
This case went on a long journey through different courtrooms.
First, it started in the regular city court (the Regional Trial Court). There, the first judge
looked at the story and said, "Because the girl thought it was a joke and wasn't scared, I won't
punish him for general child abuse. BUT, I will definitely punish him for child pornography,
because asking for the pictures is a crime no matter what."
The bad guy didn't like this, so he complained to a higher court (the Court of Appeals).
He cried about his privacy again. The higher court told him, "Stop making excuses. You are
guilty."
Finally, he went to the absolute highest court in the country (the Supreme Court). The
Supreme Court looked at everything and gave the final, permanent answer: GUILTY.
The Supreme Court ruled that he completely broke the Cybercrime Law and the AntiChild Pornography Law. Because of his bad choices, the judges sentenced him to go to prison for
a very long time—between 14 and 18 years! They also ordered him to pay a massive fine of One
Million Pesos.

V. CONCLUSION
To wrap up the whole story, the case of Cadajas v. People teaches us a very beautiful and
powerful lesson: a child's safety is the most important thing in the world.
The biggest lesson to take away is that bad guys cannot use "privacy rules" to run away
from trouble. If a brave mom finds out a bad guy is talking to her child, the court will thank the
mom and use that evidence to lock the bad guy up. The Constitution is meant to protect good
people from bad governments, not to protect predators from angry moms.
Also, this story proves that there are absolutely no excuses when it comes to keeping kids
safe on the internet. It doesn't matter if the child thought it was a fun game. It doesn't matter if
the bad guy promised never to show the pictures to anyone else. Asking a child to do grown-up,
dangerous things on camera is a huge crime. Our justice system showed that it is strong enough
and smart enough to protect our kids in the digital age, making sure the internet is a safer place
for everyone.
